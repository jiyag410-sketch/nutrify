TOPIC: Healthy Diet Basics

A healthy diet helps protect against malnutrition in all its forms and reduces risk of noncommunicable diseases such as diabetes, heart disease, stroke, and certain cancers. The foundation of a healthy diet rests on four principles: adequacy (getting enough nutrients), balance (the right proportions of food groups), moderation (not overconsuming any single nutrient), and diversity (eating a wide variety of foods).

Key recommendations for adults:
- Eat a variety of fruits, vegetables, legumes, nuts, and whole grains every day.
- Aim for at least 400g (about five servings) of fruits and vegetables per day, not counting starchy roots like potatoes.
- Limit free sugars to less than 10% of total daily energy intake, with additional benefit if reduced below 5%. Free sugars include those added to food and drinks, as well as sugars naturally present in honey, syrups, and fruit juices.
- Limit fat intake to less than 30% of total energy intake, favoring unsaturated fats (found in fish, avocado, nuts, and oils like olive and sunflower) over saturated fats (found in fatty meat, butter, and coconut oil) and industrially produced trans fats (found in some processed and fried foods).
- Keep sodium intake under 5g of salt per day (roughly one teaspoon), and choose iodized salt when possible.
- Balance calorie intake with physical activity to maintain a healthy body weight.

Breastfeeding and early nutrition play an important role in lifelong health, and dietary habits formed in childhood often carry into adulthood.

Diet is influenced by many factors beyond personal choice, including income, food prices, cultural traditions, and access to healthy food environments.

DISCLAIMER: This is general public health information, not personalized medical advice. Individuals with specific health conditions should consult a healthcare provider or registered dietitian.
