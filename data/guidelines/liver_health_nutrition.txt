TOPIC: Foods and Diet Patterns Supportive of Liver Health

The liver plays a central role in metabolism, digestion, and filtering toxins from the body. While no single food can "cure" or directly treat liver disease, certain dietary patterns are generally associated with supporting healthy liver function and reducing risk factors for conditions like fatty liver disease.

General dietary guidance for liver health:
- Limit alcohol intake, since excessive alcohol consumption is a leading cause of liver damage; for anyone with existing liver conditions, avoiding alcohol entirely is typically recommended by healthcare providers.
- Maintain a healthy body weight, since obesity and excess body fat — particularly around the abdomen — are strongly linked to non-alcoholic fatty liver disease (NAFLD), which is increasingly common in India due to rising obesity rates.
- Limit added sugars and refined carbohydrates (sugary drinks, sweets, white bread, refined flour/maida), since excess sugar intake is converted to fat in the liver and contributes to fatty liver disease.
- Reduce saturated and trans fats (fried foods, processed snacks, excess ghee/butter) and favor healthier fats from nuts, seeds, and oils in moderation.
- Increase fiber intake through vegetables, fruits, whole grains, and legumes, which supports healthy digestion and metabolic function.
- Stay adequately hydrated with water throughout the day.
- Some plant foods (garlic, leafy greens, coffee in moderation) are associated with liver health in general population studies, though these are supportive dietary patterns rather than treatments.

Important distinction: this guidance concerns liver organ health (the body's liver function), not recipes that contain animal liver as a food ingredient (such as chicken liver or lamb liver dishes) — these are different topics that should not be confused with each other.

DISCLAIMER: This is general public health information, not personalized medical advice or a diagnosis. Anyone with a diagnosed liver condition (hepatitis, fatty liver disease, cirrhosis, or other liver disorders) should follow the specific dietary guidance of their doctor or hepatologist, as needs vary significantly by condition and severity.
