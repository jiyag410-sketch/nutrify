TOPIC: Indian Dietary Guidelines Basics (ICMR-NIN 2024)

The Dietary Guidelines for Indians, published by the ICMR-National Institute of Nutrition (NIN), provide recommendations tailored to Indian eating patterns, food availability, and health needs. India faces a dual burden of malnutrition — undernutrition in some populations alongside rising obesity and diet-related chronic disease in others — so the guidelines aim to address both.

Key recommendations for adults:
- Eat a variety of foods across food groups every day: cereals and millets, pulses and legumes, vegetables, fruits, milk and milk products, and moderate amounts of fats, oils, nuts, and seeds.
- Increase intake of vegetables and legumes (pulses/dals), aiming for at least 400-500g of vegetables and fruits combined per day.
- Include millets (jowar, bajra, ragi) alongside rice and wheat, since millets offer more fiber and micronutrients and are well-suited to Indian growing conditions.
- Limit salt intake to less than 5g per day (about one teaspoon), similar to global guidance, since Indian diets often run high in sodium from pickles, papad, and processed snacks.
- Restrict added sugars to less than 10% of total daily calories; minimize sweets, sugary beverages, and ultra-processed snacks.
- Use healthy cooking oils in moderation, and vary oil sources (mustard, groundnut, sesame, rice bran) rather than relying on a single oil type; minimize deep-frying.
- Engage in regular physical activity and take steps to prevent abdominal obesity and overweight, which are common concerns in urban Indian populations.
- Stay adequately hydrated, with 8-10 glasses of water daily, and prefer options like buttermilk, coconut water, or fresh fruit (without added sugar) over sugary or highly caffeinated drinks.

The guidelines emphasize diet quality over quantity, sustainability of food choices, and nutrition needs across different life stages, including pregnancy, lactation, infancy, and childhood.

DISCLAIMER: This is general public health information, not personalized medical advice. Individuals with specific health conditions should consult a healthcare provider or registered dietitian.
