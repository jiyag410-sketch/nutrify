TOPIC: Food Groups and Balanced Meals (MyPlate Framework)

The USDA's MyPlate model divides a healthy plate into five food groups to help guide balanced eating:

1. Fruits — Whole fruits are preferred over juice. Aim to fill about a quarter of your plate with fruit at meals, or include fruit as snacks.

2. Vegetables — Aim for variety across subgroups: dark leafy greens, red and orange vegetables (carrots, peppers, tomatoes), starchy vegetables (corn, potatoes), beans and peas, and other vegetables. Vegetables should make up roughly a quarter to a third of the plate.

3. Grains — At least half of grains consumed should be whole grains (brown rice, whole wheat bread, oats, quinoa) rather than refined grains (white bread, white rice), since whole grains retain more fiber and nutrients.

4. Protein — Includes meat, poultry, seafood, eggs, beans, peas, nuts, seeds, and soy products. Seafood and plant-based proteins are encouraged as part of a varied protein intake, and lean cuts are preferred over fatty or processed meats.

5. Dairy — Includes milk, yogurt, and cheese, or fortified soy alternatives. Low-fat or fat-free options are generally recommended for adults.

General plate-building guidance:
- Make half your plate fruits and vegetables.
- Make at least half your grains whole grains.
- Vary your protein choices across the week.
- Choose lower-fat dairy or fortified alternatives.
- Limit added sugars, saturated fat, and sodium.

DISCLAIMER: This is general public health information, not personalized medical advice or a prescribed meal plan. Nutritional needs vary by age, activity level, and health status.
