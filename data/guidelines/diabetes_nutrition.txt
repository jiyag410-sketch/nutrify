TOPIC: Nutrition and Type 2 Diabetes (General Public Guidance)

Nutrition plays a central role in managing blood sugar for people with, or at risk of, type 2 diabetes. This is general public health information, not a substitute for individualized guidance from a doctor or registered dietitian.

General dietary patterns associated with better blood sugar management:
- Prioritize non-starchy vegetables (leafy greens, broccoli, peppers, cucumbers) to fill a large portion of the plate.
- Choose whole grains and high-fiber carbohydrates over refined/processed carbohydrates, since fiber slows sugar absorption.
- Include lean protein sources (poultry, fish, beans, tofu, eggs) at meals to help with satiety and blood sugar stability.
- Limit sugary beverages, refined sugars, and heavily processed snack foods, which can cause rapid blood sugar spikes.
- Pay attention to portion sizes of carbohydrate-containing foods (grains, fruit, starchy vegetables, dairy), since carbohydrates have the most direct impact on blood sugar.
- Healthy fats (nuts, seeds, olive oil, avocado) in moderation can be part of a balanced diabetes-friendly diet.
- Consistent meal timing can help some individuals manage blood sugar more predictably, though needs vary by person and medication.

This information describes general patterns and is not a diagnosis, treatment plan, or personalized meal plan. Blood sugar targets, medication interactions, and specific dietary needs vary significantly between individuals.

DISCLAIMER: This is general public health information, not personalized medical advice. Anyone managing diabetes or prediabetes should work with a healthcare provider or certified diabetes educator for individualized guidance.
