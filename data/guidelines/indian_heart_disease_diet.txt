TOPIC: Heart Disease and Cardiovascular-Friendly Diet

Cardiovascular disease (heart disease, stroke, hypertension-related complications) is a leading cause of death in India, and Indians as a population tend to develop heart disease at younger ages and with higher risk at a given body weight compared to some other populations, partly due to genetic and dietary factors.

Key dietary strategies for heart health:
- Limit saturated and trans fats: reduce ghee, butter, coconut oil, and vanaspati/dalda (hydrogenated vegetable fat) used in excess, and avoid repeatedly reheated cooking oil, which forms harmful trans fats. Favor oils like mustard, groundnut, sesame, or rice bran oil in moderate quantities.
- Reduce fried and deep-fried foods (pakoras, samosas, puris, fried snacks), which are common in Indian diets but contribute significantly to unhealthy fat intake when eaten frequently.
- Limit sodium intake to under 5g of salt per day, since high sodium intake — common from pickles, papad, namkeen, and processed foods — raises blood pressure, a major risk factor for heart disease.
- Increase fiber intake through whole grains, millets, vegetables, fruits, and legumes, which helps manage cholesterol levels.
- Include heart-healthy fats in moderation: nuts (almonds, walnuts), seeds (flaxseed, chia), and fatty fish (for non-vegetarians) provide beneficial omega-3 fatty acids.
- Limit refined carbohydrates and added sugars (white rice in excess, sweets, sugary beverages), since these contribute to obesity and metabolic syndrome, both linked to heart disease risk.
- Maintain a healthy body weight and waist circumference, since abdominal obesity is strongly linked to heart disease risk in the Indian population specifically.
- Engage in regular physical activity (at least 150 minutes of moderate activity per week, such as brisk walking), which supports healthy blood pressure, cholesterol, and weight.

Additional considerations for the Indian diet:
- Traditional Indian thalis can be heart-healthy when balanced — emphasizing dal, sabzi, salad, and a moderate portion of rice/roti — but risk becomes higher when meals are dominated by fried items, excess rice, and very limited vegetables.
- Regular health checkups including blood pressure, cholesterol (lipid profile), and blood sugar are recommended, especially given the Indian population's tendency toward earlier-onset cardiovascular risk.

DISCLAIMER: This is general public health information, not personalized medical advice or a diagnosis. Individuals with diagnosed heart disease, high cholesterol, or hypertension should follow the specific dietary and treatment guidance of their cardiologist or doctor.
