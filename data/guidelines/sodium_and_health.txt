TOPIC: Sodium, Salt, and Blood Pressure

Most people consume far more sodium than the body needs, primarily from processed and restaurant foods rather than salt added at the table. High sodium intake is strongly linked to high blood pressure (hypertension), which increases the risk of heart disease and stroke.

Key recommendations:
- Adults should aim to consume less than 2,300 mg of sodium per day (roughly one teaspoon of table salt), with an ideal target closer to 1,500 mg for many adults, especially those with high blood pressure.
- Most dietary sodium comes from packaged, processed, and restaurant foods rather than home cooking — common contributors include bread, pizza, cold cuts, soups, and savory snacks.
- Reading nutrition labels and choosing "low sodium," "reduced sodium," or "no salt added" versions of packaged foods can meaningfully reduce intake.
- Cooking at home with herbs, spices, citrus, and vinegar instead of salt is an effective way to reduce sodium while preserving flavor.
- Potassium-rich foods (bananas, leafy greens, beans, sweet potatoes) can help offset some effects of sodium on blood pressure, though this should not replace reducing sodium intake directly.

People with hypertension, kidney disease, or heart conditions often need stricter sodium limits and should follow guidance from their healthcare provider.

DISCLAIMER: This is general public health information, not personalized medical advice. Individuals managing blood pressure or kidney conditions should consult a healthcare provider before making significant dietary changes.
