TOPIC: PCOS (Polycystic Ovary Syndrome) and Diet

PCOS is a common hormonal and metabolic condition affecting a significant share of Indian women of reproductive age. It involves insulin resistance in many cases, along with hormonal imbalance that can affect menstrual cycles, weight, and fertility. There is no single universal "PCOS diet," but certain dietary patterns are widely recommended to help manage symptoms.

Key dietary principles:
- Favor low glycemic index (low-GI) foods, which cause a slower, smaller rise in blood sugar. Examples include millets (ragi, jowar, bajra), whole grains, legumes/dals, and most vegetables, over refined carbohydrates like white rice, white bread, and sugary foods.
- Prioritize fiber-rich foods (vegetables, fruits with skin, whole grains, legumes) which support digestion and help slow sugar absorption.
- Include adequate protein at each meal (dals, paneer, curd/yogurt, eggs, fish, lean meats for non-vegetarians) to support satiety and hormonal balance.
- Limit sugary foods, sweetened beverages, and heavily processed/fried snacks, since these can worsen insulin resistance.
- Include healthy fats in moderation (nuts, seeds, mustard or olive oil) rather than eliminating fat entirely.
- Gradual, modest weight loss (around 5-10% of body weight, for those who are overweight) has been shown to improve menstrual regularity and insulin sensitivity in many women with PCOS — rapid or extreme dieting is not recommended.
- Regular physical activity (walking, yoga, strength training) supports insulin sensitivity and weight management alongside diet.

PCOS and thyroid connection:
Thyroid disorders (particularly hypothyroidism) occur at higher rates among women with PCOS and share overlapping symptoms (weight gain, irregular periods, fatigue). Adequate iodine intake (iodized salt, dairy, seafood) supports thyroid function, and anyone with symptoms of both conditions should have their thyroid function (TSH) tested alongside PCOS evaluation.

A typical Indian vegetarian approach might include: millet or vegetable-based breakfasts, dal with sabzi and a small portion of rice or roti for lunch, sprouts or curd-based snacks, and a light dinner with vegetables and protein.

DISCLAIMER: This is general dietary information, not personalized medical advice or a diagnosis. PCOS should be diagnosed and managed by a healthcare provider (gynecologist or endocrinologist), and dietary changes should ideally be personalized with a registered dietitian, especially if other conditions like thyroid disorders or diabetes are also present.
